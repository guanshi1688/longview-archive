# Reading After

This series advances one historical phenomenon and one observable result.

## 1. The phenomenon

Before large-scale foreign investment, China had already experienced prolonged population mobility, grassroots reorganization, and the wider diffusion of schools, public health, technical knowledge, and productive order.

These processes do not mean that every policy was correct. They do not imply that every participant acted voluntarily or benefited personally.

## 2. The result

After reform and opening, China did more than absorb foreign factories. It displayed an ability to train people at scale, expand production geographically, accumulate process knowledge, form suppliers, and reproduce productive nodes.

This industrial result indicates that foreign capital did not encounter an untouched traditional agrarian society.

## 3. What the series has not proved

It has not proved that:

- every policy adopted in the decades after 1949 deserves approval;
- every migration and personal loss was the only possible road to industrialization;
- Chinese productive capability arose from an innate national character;
- state mobilization by itself guarantees industrial success.

## 4. The causal sequence restored here

> Older rural society depended heavily on local social relations  
> → prolonged warfare and cross-regional mobility loosened local confinement  
> → the new state reorganized populations through schools, public health, grassroots institutions, factories, and personnel movement  
> → modern productive order spread from a limited number of urban nodes into a much wider society  
> → reform and foreign investment encountered a broader base of absorptive capacity  
> → Chinese industrialization displayed learning and reproductive powers that cannot be explained by capital input alone.

## 5. Final judgment

Industrialization is not a process in which money arrives and machinery is purchased.

It first requires a society to form common knowledge, organizational discipline, technical transmission, and intergenerational reproduction, so that ordinary people can continuously become new producers.

> **China’s later industrial results show that the preparation of people before the arrival of foreign capital was not an irrelevant prehistory. It was one of the deep conditions that made industrialization possible.**
