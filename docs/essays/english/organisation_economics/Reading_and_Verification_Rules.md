# Reading and Verification Rules｜The Economics of Productive Organisation

## The Place of This Book

*Civilizational Structural Algorithm* is the historical, generative framework for organisational forms. *The Economics of Productive Organisation* explains how those forms operate after entering one material economic circuit. Short essays, policy commentary, and case studies are projections. A projection does not automatically supersede a canonical text merely because it appears later. If real-world evidence calls for revision of the framework, state precisely which causal link requires revision.

## Rule 1｜Material Production as a First Premise Does Not Make Production Morally Superior to Consumption

Material production is a common prerequisite for life and exchange. Consumption is part of using output, sustaining people, and forming demand for the next round. “Beginning with productive capacity” is not a reason to exclude households, social absorption, or interfaces from economics.

## Rule 2｜Internal Pressure on People Is Neither National Character nor an Unlimited Duty to Work

The phrase explains how continuous production's requirements reach people's schedules, training, cooperation, and responsibilities. It does not by itself establish the historical origin of any culture or imply that workers must bear unlimited consequences of failure. Judge whether it preserves capability by also examining income, health, rest, skills, and reproduction across generations. The particular histories of myth, ethics, belief, and institutions belong to independent projection essays using their own evidence; they cannot be deduced as historical fact from the manual's terminology.

## Rule 3｜Production-Bearing and Interface-Oriented Are Not Nationalities

Chinese, European, and American historical material may help identify organisational priorities, but behaviour must not be assigned to innate national character. State the industry, period, responsible actor, and crisis conditions. Both directions may coexist within one organisation, and the predominant direction may change.

## Rule 4｜Distinguish Necessary Conditions, Sufficient Conditions, and Outcomes

Roads may be among industrialisation's necessary conditions. Building roads is not sufficient for industrialisation. Building a factory makes production possible but does not guarantee sustainable orders. Economic growth can raise household income without automatically improving the sharing of risk or support for consumption. Any claim of inevitable success or failure must identify the intervening links.

## Rule 5｜Verify Production, Access, Sales, and Reproduction Separately

A capability faces at least four distinct tests: does it physically exist; can a given actor obtain it on time; are effective orders sufficient; and can maintenance and next-round investment be funded after this round? One must not be substituted for another.

## Rule 6｜Neither Price nor System Contribution Cancels the Other

Market prices, profits, and contracts are real coordinating and settlement mechanisms. Conditions outside an individual ledger, external effects, and long-term recovery capacity may also be real. “Not fully priced” does not mean costless; “no direct price” does not mean no contribution. Identify time horizon, beneficiaries, alternatives, and opportunity cost.

## Rule 7｜Protecting a Function Does Not Mean Preserving Its Original Operator

Where a service cannot stop, consider whether the operator or equipment may change, debts may be restructured, or procurement contracts rewritten. Where a company may exit, consider the transfer of essential supply and people. Bankruptcy, support, and reorganisation are conditional organisational tools, not moral labels.

## Rule 8｜Use the Restaurant–Factory Analogy Carefully

The restaurant's existing customers and incumbent expansion of seating explain order allocation. Industrial production additionally involves capital thresholds, technology, certification, clusters, and strategic procurement. An analogy illustrates one causal link; it does not substitute for industry evidence.

## Rule 9｜Successful Feedback Must Be Observable

Do not merely assert that history inevitably enters a new stage. Ask which capabilities accumulated, which constraints changed, who began carrying functions previously outsourced, and what happened to costs and orders. Feedback is a mechanism to verify, not a universal word that predicts every outcome.

## Rule 10｜Label Different Levels of Statement Separately

- **Definition:** the stated scope of a term in this manual.
- **Mechanism:** a causal relationship that may recur under specified conditions.
- **Historical explanation:** a claim about how a group actually acted in a given period.
- **Contemporary hypothesis:** an explanation proposed for an event not yet adequately tested.
- **Normative choice:** a decision involving rights, budgets, law, and social preferences.

Do not treat a definition as evidence, a structural explanation as an already-proven historical claim, or a contemporary hypothesis as an unconditional policy command.

## Rule 11｜Counterexamples Must Address the Claimed Causal Link

“The West also mobilises industry” and “China also has financial markets” do not, by themselves, negate two organisational directions. If contracts in a particular industry already secure maintenance and public responsibility, that fact may directly correct a local claim that “interfaces can never maintain such capability.” Do not allocate counterexamples by national name; match them to specific propositions.

Likewise, the validity of one explanatory pathway does not invalidate every other mechanism. Examine alternatives as needed: technological disruption, resource prices, political decisions, military risk, population change, or particular management failures.

## Rule 12｜Verify Numbers, History, and Current Conditions Separately

This is a structural manual. It does not by itself establish any country's present industrial output, effects of particular subsidies, disaster response, market shares, or future developments. Projection essays using such facts should check sources to the intended publication's standard, identify time period and population, and distinguish material from inference. Do not invent historical numerical indicators to make the framework appear complete.

## Rule 13｜Format Serves the Argument

Use one `#` title per chapter and `##` for numbered sections. Introduce terms in full at first use. Do not force a bold maxim into every section. Use diagrams for causation or branching that ordinary prose cannot show clearly. Do not edit by fixed paragraph length, contrived fragments, unnecessary English jargon, or an “AI detector.” Public projections need editing for their own audience and venue, not reverse changes to the working manuscript.

## Final Check

For any passage explaining an economic matter, one should be able to identify the real condition of life or production at issue, the actor with access or responsibility, the observable causal link, the type of failure or feedback, and whether the next round can continue. If these cannot be identified, narrow the claim or obtain more evidence.
